Four ones in a row — 11.11 — have become one of the most important dates in global commerce. For a founder, it's a ready-made launch moment with built-in buyer attention. Here's what it is and how to use it, even if you're a tiny business far from Asia.

What is 11.11?

Singles' Day began in the 1990s among university students in Nanjing, China, as a light-hearted celebration of being single — the four ones representing four single people. In 2009 Alibaba turned the date into an online shopping festival, often called Double 11. It has since grown into what is widely reported as the world's largest online shopping event, with platforms now running multi-week campaigns around the date rather than a single day.

Why it matters outside China

  • Global marketplaces and brands in Southeast Asia, Europe and beyond increasingly run 11.11 promotions.
  • It opens the November–December peak season, two to three weeks before Black Friday and Cyber Monday.
  • Shoppers are already in deal-hunting mode, which lowers the effort required to get attention.
  • For businesses sourcing from China, the period also affects factory lead times and shipping capacity — plan inventory early.

A six-week 11.11 plan

Six weeks out

  • Decide your offer: a discount, a bundle, a limited edition, a launch of a new product or founding-member pricing for a digital product.
  • Check your margins with the Break-even Calculator — discounts must still leave room for profit.

Four weeks out

  • Build a dedicated 11.11 landing page with a countdown.
  • Start collecting early-access sign-ups.
  • Order inventory and confirm shipping cut-offs if you sell physical goods.

Two weeks out

  • Prepare creative and copy for every channel.
  • Brief partners and affiliates.
  • Test checkout, discount codes and site speed on mobile.

On 11.11

  • Email early-access subscribers first.
  • Go live at midnight local time or at the start of your main market's morning.
  • Post updates through the day and answer questions quickly.

After

  • Onboard new customers carefully — deal-driven buyers churn faster without a strong first experience.
  • Roll learnings into your Black Friday and Cyber Monday plans.

Keep it ethical

Use real reference prices, honest scarcity and clear terms. Fake countdowns and inflated "was" prices erode trust and may break consumer-protection laws in many countries.
