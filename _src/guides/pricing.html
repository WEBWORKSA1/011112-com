Pricing is the most powerful lever most founders never pull. A modest price increase can do more for revenue than months of extra marketing — yet many early products are priced by copying a competitor or picking a number that "feels fair". Here's a better way.

1. Price on value, not cost

Your costs set a floor; your customer's value sets the ceiling. Ask interviewees what the problem costs them today in time, money or risk. If your product saves a business ten hours a month, a price that captures a fraction of that value is easy to justify.

2. Pick a value metric

A value metric is the unit you charge for — seats, projects, contacts, invoices, API calls, AI credits. The best metric grows as the customer gets more value, so revenue expands naturally with successful customers.

Product typeCommon value metrics
Team collaborationSeats / active users
Marketing / CRMContacts or emails sent
AI toolsCredits, generations or usage tiers
Finance / opsTransactions, invoices or entities

3. Design good–better–best tiers

Three tiers work well for most early products:

  • Starter — for individuals; low commitment, limited usage.
  • Pro — the plan you expect most customers to choose. Make it the obvious best value.
  • Business — higher limits, collaboration, priority support, admin controls.

Differentiate tiers by usage and by features that matter more to larger customers (permissions, integrations, support), not by crippling the core experience.

4. Offer annual plans

An annual option, typically discounted by about two months, improves cash flow and reduces churn. Cash collected upfront extends your runway — see runway and burn rate explained.

5. Free trial or freemium?

Free trials suit products that deliver value quickly to a defined buyer. Freemium suits products with viral or network effects and very low cost to serve. AI products with real usage costs should cap free usage tightly so free users don't burn your margin.

6. Test prices safely

  1. Raise prices for new customers only and keep existing customers on their current plan (grandfathering).
  2. Change one thing at a time and give it enough traffic to judge.
  3. Watch conversion and revenue per visitor — a lower conversion rate at a higher price can still mean more revenue.
  4. Talk to customers who didn't buy; price is often a proxy for unclear value.

7. Check the unit economics

Price must support your acquisition costs. A common benchmark is LTV:CAC of at least 3:1 and CAC payback under 12 months. Enter your price, churn and acquisition cost into the MRR & LTV Projector and the Break-even Calculator to see whether the model works.

Quick checklist

  • Value metric chosen and explained on the pricing page
  • Three clear tiers with a highlighted recommended plan
  • Annual plan available
  • Price test scheduled within 90 days of launch
