A launch isn't a day. It's a four-week sequence that ends with a day. Founders who treat launch as a single post usually get a single spike and silence. Founders who plan the sequence get a spike, a list and a pipeline. Here's the plan.

T-30 days: pick the moment and the offer

  • Choose a date that avoids major holidays in your market — or deliberately aligns with a commerce moment like 11.11 or Black Friday. The Commerce Launch Calendar lists upcoming dates.
  • Define the launch offer: founding-member pricing, a bonus, extended trial or limited spots. Give people a reason to act on the day.
  • Set one goal, such as 100 activated users or 25 paying customers.

T-21 days: build the waitlist

  • Publish a pre-launch page with an email signup.
  • Share progress publicly ("build in public") two or three times a week.
  • Personally invite everyone from your validation interviews.

T-14 days: prepare assets

  • A 60–90 second demo video and 3–5 screenshots.
  • Short and long descriptions, a one-line tagline and FAQs.
  • Launch posts drafted for each channel, adapted to each platform's style.
  • A test of the full signup and payment flow on mobile and desktop.

T-7 days: line up channels and partners

  • Pick 3–5 channels where your customers already are: product directories, niche communities, newsletters, social platforms, relevant podcasts.
  • Read each community's self-promotion rules and follow them.
  • Ask partners, early users and friends in the industry to share on the day — give them ready-made text and images.
  • Schedule your launch email to the waitlist.

Launch day

  1. Start early in your main market's time zone; many directories reset daily.
  2. Email the waitlist first — they're your warmest audience.
  3. Post across channels in a planned order, not all at once.
  4. Reply to every comment quickly and personally. Engagement drives visibility.
  5. Watch the funnel: visits, sign-ups, activations. Fix any broken step immediately.
  6. Share updates through the day — milestones, thank-yous, behind the scenes.

T+1 to T+7: convert attention into customers

  • Send a welcome sequence to every new sign-up that leads them to the first valuable action.
  • Personally contact the most engaged users and ask what almost stopped them signing up.
  • Publish a launch recap — numbers and lessons attract a second wave.
  • Close the launch offer when you said you would. Credibility compounds.

After the launch

Launch-day traffic fades; channels that keep working don't. Pick the one or two channels that produced activated users and build a repeatable routine around them. That's the bridge from the Launch stage to the Earn stage of the 0‑1111‑2 Method.
