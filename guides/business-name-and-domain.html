How to Choose a Business Name and Domain

A practical five-test framework for choosing a business name, checking trademarks, picking a domain extension and deciding when a premium domain is worth the investment.

By the 011112.com Editorial Team · Updated November 2026 · 7 min read

Your name and domain appear on every invoice, ad, email and conversation. A good one makes everything a little easier; a bad one creates friction forever. Here's how to choose well without spending weeks on it.

The five tests

  1. The radio test. Say the name to someone. Can they spell it and type the domain without help? Creative misspellings fail this test.
  2. The length test. Shorter is easier to remember and type. Aim for two syllables to three and fewer than about 12 characters where you can.
  3. The meaning test. Check the name in your target markets' languages and slang. Avoid accidental negative meanings.
  4. The trademark test. Search your country's trademark office database (and those of key markets) for identical or confusingly similar marks in your category. This is the test founders skip most often, and the most expensive to fail.
  5. The growth test. Will the name still fit if you add products or markets? "Toronto Dog Walkers" is clear but hard to expand.

Choosing a domain extension

.com remains the default most people type first and trust most broadly. Country extensions (.ca, .in, .co.uk) build local trust. Newer extensions like .ai, .io and .app suit tech products but can cost more to renew. If you use a non-.com extension, consider buying the .com later when you can afford it — or at least make sure it isn't owned by a direct competitor.

When a premium domain is worth it

A short, memorable domain is one of the few startup assets that can hold its value. It can reduce marketing friction, increase direct type-in traffic and boost trust. It's usually worth considering when:

  • You'll rely on word of mouth, radio, podcasts or offline marketing.
  • You're selling internationally and want a language-neutral name.
  • Trust is central to your business (finance, health, B2B).

Use an escrow service for any domain purchase, confirm the domain's history and check it isn't subject to a trademark dispute.

A 30-minute naming sprint

  1. Write 10 words that describe your customer's desired outcome.
  2. Run the strongest through the Business Name Generator in two styles.
  3. Shortlist five; run the five tests.
  4. Check domains and social handles.
  5. Run a trademark search on your top two, then decide.
